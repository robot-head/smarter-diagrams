1. Executive Summary & Problem Statement
Text-to-diagram tools like Mermaid achieved ubiquitous adoption across software engineering because plain-text definitions are fast to type, trivial for Large Language Models (LLMs) to synthesize, and cleanly diffable in Git. However, Mermaid's visual output deteriorates rapidly past 10 to 15 nodes. Because it relies on legacy Sugiyama-style layered Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) assumptions (dagre), treats nodes as point-masses without explicit perimeter ports, lacks collision-free cartographic label placement, and re-shuffles layouts unpredictably upon minor edits, engineers are forced to abandon text-as-code whenever they need publication-grade or executive-ready architecture diagrams.
Core Architectural Thesis
Achieving Mermaid's five-second authoring simplicity alongside studio-grade visual legibility requires decoupling semantic topology from spatial synthesis. This design specification defines a next-generation computer science diagramming engine built on four foundational breakthroughs:


1. Progressive LL(2) Surface DSL + Lossless Concrete Syntax Tree (CST): A three-line Mermaid-simple sketch is already a valid canonical model, scaling progressively to 5-tier port constraints, cross-cutting Gestalt zones, and lossless bidirectional canvas @pin edits without corrupting comments or formatting.
2. Three-Stage Sub-16ms Bilevel Layout Solver: Decomposes NP-hard layout optimization into (a) macro-shape and port assignment, (b) mental-map-preserving separable quadratic compaction (VPSC) with scanline orthogonal visibility routing, and (c) 0–1 integer linear label slot selection paired with forward-mode Wasm autodiff Signed Distance Field (SDF) micro-polish.
3. Cognitive Notation Intelligence ("Physics of Notations"): Bakes perceptual discriminability, OKLCH tonal hierarchy, optical crossing bridges, and dual-coded semantic shapes into zero-configuration defaults.
4. Executable & Epistemic Diagramming (Phase 2 Extensions): Elevates diagrams from passive drawings into live computational instruments supporting 60Hz fluid-queueing wind tunnels, OpenTelemetry/AST "Phantom Drift" reconciliation, bounded SAT/SMT counterexample choreography, 2.5D stratigraphic stacks, and bitemporal "Spatial Git" superposition.
2. Research Foundation & Literature Review
Modern diagram legibility sits at the intersection of four active computer science research streams: declarative spatial languages, orthogonal/compound graph drawing, cognitive notation science, and multimodal visual critique.
2.1 Declarative Relations & Optimization-Based Synthesis (HCI & Programming Languages)
* Pollock et al., "Bluefish: Composing Diagrams with Declarative Relations" (MIT CSAIL, ACM UIST 2024): Demonstrates why strict hierarchical UI component trees fail on computer science diagrams (such as compiler parse trees, memory layouts, and overlapping network topologies). Bluefish replaces rigid parent-child trees with a compound relational scenegraph grounded in Gestalt perceptual relations (alignment, containment, background hulls, connectors), enabling overlapping visual structures to share nodes cleanly without manual bounding-box arithmetic.
* Ye et al., "Penrose: From Mathematical Notation to Beautiful Diagrams" (CMU, ACM SIGGRAPH 2020) & Estep, Ni, Sunshine et al., "Rose: Composable Autodiff for the Interactive Web" (CMU, 2024): Introduces a clean architectural triad separating domain vocabulary (Domain), terse declarative topology (Substance), and visual mapping rules (Style). Diagrams compile into numerical optimization problems—jointly optimizing proximity, label repulsion, and contrast—which Rose accelerates by orders of magnitude in the browser via WebAssembly automatic differentiation.
* Bourgault et al. / Cartokit, "Direct Manipulation and Natural Language Programming, Together at Last?" (OOPSLA 2026): Introduces a shared structured edit algebra uniting code/NL synthesis with direct canvas manipulation. Empirical user studies demonstrate that engineers perform over 93% of spatial layout refinements via direct canvas manipulation while reserving text/NL for parameterizing and repeating structural patterns—proving pure text-only workflows fight human spatial cognition.
2.2 Compound Graph Drawing, Ports & Orthogonal Routing
* Andreola, Di Battista, Patrignani et al., "A Walk on the Wild Side: A Shape-First Methodology for Orthogonal Drawings" (GD 2025): Introduces domus, a shape-first orthogonal drawing algorithm that consistently outperforms classic Topology-Shape-Metrics (TSM) pipelines (such as OGDF) on bend minimization and visual readability across small-to-medium graphs—the exact regime of software architecture diagrams.
* Domrös, von Hanxleden, Spönemann, Rüegg, & Schulze, "The Eclipse Layout Kernel (ELK)" (Kiel University, 2023 / GD 2024) & Schulze et al., "Drawing Layered Graphs with Port Constraints" (JVLC 2014): Establishes why CS block diagrams cannot model nodes as point-masses. ELK introduces explicit 5-tier port constraints (pinning True/False branches to distinct diamond sides or routing SQL foreign keys to exact row slots), recursive compound containers, and Coffman–Graham bounded-width layering so wide architectures pack into balanced 2D grids.
* Alexander Wang / Terrastruct, "TALA (Terrastruct's AutoLayout Algorithm)" (Open-Sourced Sept 2026, MPL-2.0): Purpose-built for software architecture rather than generic mathematical networks. TALA evaluates candidate seeds against a human-tuned architectural scoring function and supports hybrid coordinate locking, letting humans or AI models pin specific nodes in 2D space while automatically routing unpinned subgraphs.
2.3 Cognitive Perception & Visual Notation Science
* Daniel Moody, "The 'Physics' of Notations: Toward a Scientific Basis for Constructing Visual Notations in Software Engineering" (IEEE TSE 2009; extended in 2025–2026 eye-tracking & notation studies): Codifies nine empirical principles for cognitive effectiveness:
   * Perceptual Discriminability: Maximize visual distance across multiple retinal variables (silhouette, fill, stroke texture, weight, iconography); novices routinely confuse ER and flowchart nodes when shapes vary only within the quadrilateral family.
   * Semiotic Clarity & Graphic Economy: Enforce a 1:1 correspondence between semantic concepts and visual symbols while bounding distinct symbol vocabulary size.
   * Dual Coding & Cognitive Manageability: Pair concise text labels with purposeful structural icons to expand working memory, and decompose dense graphs into navigable semantic zoom levels.
* Mooney, Purchase, Wybrow, & Kobourov, "The Multi-Dimensional Landscape of Graph Drawing Metrics" & "The Perception of Stress in Graph Drawings" (2024): Quantifies which geometric metrics govern human path-tracing accuracy: minimizing edge crossings first, maximizing crossing angles toward 90 degrees when crossings are unavoidable, minimizing connector bends, maintaining uniform edge lengths, and minimizing topological stress.
2.4 VLM Aesthetic Critique & Dual Human-AI Readability (2025–2026 Frontier)
* Tianfu Wang et al., "EvoDesign: Agentic Editable Diagram Creation via Design Expertise Evolution" (NeurIPS 2026): Formulates diagramming over a machine-actionable, object-level editable canvas schema refined iteratively through rendered visual feedback, backed by a three-tier Hierarchical Expertise Memory (local repairs → domain conventions → universal density/modularity principles).
* PaperBanana, "Automating Academic Illustration for AI Scientists" (Jan 2026): Benchmarks NeurIPS methodology diagrams (PaperBananaBench) with a Visualizer + Critic agent loop, proving that raster diffusion models hallucinate dense graph topology whereas code-backed layout paired with VLM visual critique guarantees both structural faithfulness and aesthetic polish.
* Di Bartolomeo et al., "Do Graph Drawing Aesthetics Matter for AI?" (Computer Graphics Forum, June 2026) & Miller, Wallinger, Kobourov et al., "Exploring MLLMs' Perception of Network Visualization Principles" (IEEE TVCG, July 2026): Demonstrates that Multimodal LLMs rely on human-like visual proxies (node crowding, edge overlap) when parsing diagrams, validating unified legibility optimization for both human engineers and AI coding agents.

	4. Core Seven-Pillar System Architecture
1. Progressive "Zero-to-Model" Syntax: Three lines of plain a -> b: label text render immediately with zero boilerplate, while compiling underneath into a persistent semantic graph model. Multiple views (view checkout_flow { include gateway -> auth -> * }) project from one canonical source of truth, eliminating documentation drift across Context, Container, and Sequence diagrams.
2. Two-Stage Hybrid Layout Pipeline: Combines discrete combinatorial routing (shape-first orthogonal domus + ELK 5-tier ports + Coffman–Graham aspect-ratio packing) with a continuous Penrose/Rose-style Wasm autodiff pass that polishes label offsets, channel symmetry, and padding halos.
3. Mental-Map Stability via Elastic VPSC Anchoring: Eliminates random-seed layout jumps by anchoring surviving nodes to prior relative orthogonal orderings and breaking ties deterministically via DSL declaration line order.
4. Gestalt Relational Overlays (Beyond Strict Trees): Adopts a compound relational scenegraph so cross-cutting concerns—security scopes, Raft quorums, distributed traces—render as smooth, borderless Euler contour hulls (zone) behind nodes without fighting strict tree containment (vpc_prod).
5. Opinionated Editorial Typography & "Physics of Notations" Defaults: Infers high-discriminability silhouettes from types/keywords (broker/kafka → partitioned log; postgres → tabular port block), replaces heavy wireframe borders with OKLCH tonal elevation, and renders optical crossing bridges and inline pill knockouts.
6. Lossless Bidirectional Editing (Canvas ↔ Text CST): Engineers author topology in text and drag nodes, ports, or channels directly on the canvas. Gestures synthesize semantic constraints (align row, container-relative @pin) spliced surgically into a lossless Red-Green CST so comments and whitespace survive byte-for-byte.
7. Semantic Zoom, Trace Playback & Aesthetic Linter / MCP Server: Pairs crisp static SVG/PDF export with interactive semantic zoom, step-through protocol animation, a built-in heuristic/VLM aesthetic linter, and a native Model Context Protocol (MCP) interface for AI coding agents.
5. Mathematical Solver & Multi-Objective Aesthetic Cost Specification
Solving port-aware orthogonal routing, mental-map stability, and collision-free typography as a monolithic Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Program (MINLP) is NP-hard and far too slow for interactive 60fps authoring. The layout engine decomposes a global aesthetic energy functional into a three-stage bilevel solver pipeline targeting sub-16ms incremental execution in WebAssembly.

           + λ_lbl  · J_label(L, p, R)

	5.1 Port-Aware Orthogonal Routing (J_ortho)
Every incident edge endpoint attaches to an explicit port k ∈ P_v on node v with bounding box [x_v - w_v/2, x_v + w_v/2] × [y_v - h_v/2, y_v + h_v/2].
Five-Tier Port Constraint Lattice
1. FREE: Side s_k ∈ {N, E, S, W} and tangential offset t_k are solver variables; s_k selects the cardinal sector facing the target centroid to eliminate up to 2 bends.
2. FIXED_SIDE: s_k = s̄_k is fixed (e.g., E for outputs, W for inputs, S for error/DLQ branches); perimeter order along s̄_k remains free.
3. FIXED_ORDER: Clockwise perimeter rank enforces linear separation t_{k1} + δ_port ≤ t_{k2} whenever rank(k1) < rank(k2).
4. FIXED_RATIO: Tangential coordinate locks proportionally to side length L_s: t_k = ρ_k · L_s for ρ_k ∈ (0, 1), scaling cleanly when containers resize.
5. FIXED_POS: Exact offset t_k = t̄_k (required for SQL/ER foreign-key row anchors and memory-layout pointer slots).
Hard Stub, Corner Clearance & Dynamic Node Expansion Rules
Every port k on side s_k with outward unit normal n(s_k) ∈ {(0,-1), (1,0), (0,1), (-1,0)} enforces a mandatory straight exit stub prior to its first 90-degree bend:t
q_{e,0} = π(u, k),    q_{e,1} = π(u, k) + d_stub · n(s_k),    d_stub ≥ δ_margin
t_min(s_k) + δ_corner ≤ t_k ≤ L_{s_k} - δ_corner,             |t_{k_a} - t_{k_b}| ≥ δ_port
Dynamic Perimeter Sizing: L_s ≥ (|P_{v,s}| - 1) · δ_port + 2 · δ_corner

#### Orthogonal Visibility Graph & Route Objective
Inflating every node bounding box by `δ_margin` forms open obstacle interiors `B°_v`. Sweeping horizontal and vertical scan-rays from all padded obstacle corners and port stub tips `q_{e,1}` constructs the **Orthogonal Visibility Graph** `VG = (V_VG, E_VG)`. Each polyline route `γ_e = (q_{e,0}, q_{e,1}, ..., q_{e,m_e})` minimizes:


```text
J_ortho(Π, R) = ∑_{e ∈ E} [ w_len · ∑_{j=1}^{m_e} ‖q_{e,j} - q_{e,j-1}‖₁
                          + w_bend · (m_e - 1)
                          + w_back · I_back(γ_e)
                          + w_center · δ_dev(e) ]
              + ∑_{{e, e'}} [ w_cross · χ(γ_e, γ_{e'}) + w_ovlp · Ω(γ_e, γ_{e'}) ]

* Admissible Direction-Aware A* Heuristic: Over directed visibility states (v, d_in) toward target stub (t, d_target), the exact lower bound is h(v, d_in) = ‖v - t‖₁ + w_bend · b_min(v, d_in, t, d_target), where b_min ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3, 4} counts the minimum mandatory 90-degree turns needed to align d_in with -d_target across displacement (t - v).
* Hyperedge Trunk Sharing & L ++ S ++ R Channel Nudging: Proper crossings χ(γ_e, γ_{e'}) evaluate to 0 for routes sharing a common hyperedge trunk. Collinear segments traversing a shared visibility alley are partitioned by entry turn direction—Left (L), Straight (S), Right (R)—into ordered parallel tracks O = L ++ S ++ R spaced by δ_lane via 1D VPSC centering.
5.2 Mental-Map Stability & Separable VPSC Compaction (J_mental)
Across incremental edits from G^(t-1) to G^(t), let V_∩ = V^(t) ∩ V^(t-1) denote surviving nodes with prior centroids p⁰ = {(x_v⁰, y_v⁰)}. Mental-map preservation combines four complementary terms:


J_mental(p, Π | S^(t-1)) = min_{Δ ∈ ℝ²} ∑_{v ∈ V_∩} ω_v · ‖p_v - (p_v⁰ + Δ)‖₂²
                         + μ_ord  · D_τ(p, p⁰)
                         + μ_prox · ∑_{(u,v) ∈ E_DT⁰} ( ‖p_u - p_v‖₁ / ‖p_u⁰ - p_v⁰‖₁ - 1 )²
                         + μ_dsl  · I_model(p)

1. Translation-Invariant Anchoring (Δ-Adjusted L2): Discounts rigid global viewport shifts. Explicit @pin nodes receive ω_v = 10⁵, surviving hub nodes scale with deg(v), and newly added nodes receive ω_v = 0.
2. Orthogonal Ordering Distance (D_τ): Counts pairwise Kendall-τ inversions in x and y across V_∩, ensuring a node previously above-left of a peer stays above-left unless rewiring justifies a flip.
3. Delaunay Proximity Preservation: Penalizes neighborhood stretching across edges E_DT⁰ of the prior layout's Delaunay triangulation.
4. DSL Declaration-Order Tie-Breaking (I_model): Penalizes rank inversions against source-file declaration index decl(u) < decl(v), making fresh layouts 100% seed-free and Git-deterministic.
Elasticized Separable VPSC Quadratic Program
Compaction decomposes into horizontal (x) and vertical (y) active-set Variable Placement with Separation Constraints (VPSC) passes via red-black tree scanline sweep, separating hard geometric validity from elastic mental-map slacks ξ_{uv}^x ≥ 0:


minimize_{x, ξ^x}   ∑_{v ∈ V_∩} ω_v · (x_v - x̃_v⁰)²  +  λ_comp · ∑_{(u,v) ∈ E} |x_u - x_v|  +  M_flip · ∑_{(u,v) ∈ O_x⁰} ξ_{uv}^x


subject to:
  1. Hard Non-Overlap:       x_u + (w_u + w_v)/2 + g_pad ≤ x_v          (adjacent scanline intervals)
  2. Compound Containment:   x_{C,left} + p_in + w_v/2 ≤ x_v ≤ x_{C,right} - p_in - w_v/2
  3. Zero-Bend Port Align:   -ζ_e ≤ π_y(u, k1) - π_y(v, k2) ≤ ζ_e       (straightens 2-bend jogs into 0-bend wires)
  4. Elastic Mental Order:   x_u ≤ x_v + ξ_{uv}^x,   ξ_{uv}^x ≥ 0       (flips only if topological savings > M_flip)
5.3 Collision-Free Label Placement & Differentiable SDF Polish (J_label)
Stage 3A: Discrete Candidate 0–1 Integer Linear Program (ZOLP)
Each labelable feature i ∈ F generates K_i discrete candidate boxes {B_{i,1}, ..., B_{i,K_i}} (4 Imhof-ranked external port quadrants; straight-segment edge candidates scored by Centeredness, Aboveness, ideal perpendicular clearance d_ideal, and corner-straddle avoidance) plus a guaranteed feasible fallback inline pill-cutout state B_{i,0}:


minimize_{α ∈ {0,1}}   ∑_{i ∈ F} ∑_{k=0}^{K_i} [ c_{i,k}^prior + w_edge_occ · Area(B_{i,k} ∩ R_{≠i}) + w_pill · I(k = 0) ] · α_{i,k}


subject to:
  ∑_{k=0}^{K_i} α_{i,k} = 1                       ∀ i ∈ F
  α_{i,k} + α_{j,r} ≤ 1                           ∀ overlapping pairs B_{i,k} ∩ B_{j,r} ≠ ∅
  α_{i,k} = 0                                     whenever B_{i,k} intersects any node B_v or port stub Π

Connected components of the R-tree overlap conflict graph rarely exceed 8–12 contesting labels in orthogonal layouts and solve to exact optimality in under 1 millisecond. When fallback α_{i,0} = 1 activates, the renderer draws a 4px background halo knockout pill so connector strokes break cleanly around glyph ascenders and descenders.
Stage 3B: Continuous Differentiable SDF Micro-Polish
With discrete topology and slots locked, 15–25 iterations of forward-mode Wasm autodiff L-BFGS refine continuous tangential label slide parameters s_i ∈ [0, 1] and floating visibility-channel coordinates u_c ∈ [u_{c,min}, u_{c,max}] over a smooth Signed Distance Field (SDF) objective:


E_polish(s, u) = ∑_{A ≠ B} w_repel · softplus_β²(τ_clear - SDF(A, B))
               + ∑_{i ∈ F} w_anchor · (s_i - s_{i,init})²
               + ∑_{c ∈ C} w_sym · ( u_c - (u_{c,min} + u_{c,max}) / 2 )²

where softplus_β(z) = (1/β) · ln(1 + exp(β·z)) provides C²-smooth clearance repulsion.

	6.2 Formal LL(2) Surface Grammar (EBNF)
Diagram         ::= Statement* EOF
Statement       ::= ZoneDecl | AlignDecl | DistributeDecl | FlowDecl
                  | LayoutBlock | EdgeStmt | NodeOrContainer


NodeOrContainer ::= QualifiedId (":" LabelText)? AttrList? Annotation* Block? StmtEnd
Block           ::= "{" (PortDecl | Statement)* "}"
PortDecl        ::= "port" Ident (":" Ident)? AttrList? Annotation* StmtEnd


EdgeStmt        ::= Endpoint (EdgeOp Endpoint)+ (":" LabelText)? AttrList? Annotation* StmtEnd
EdgeOp          ::= "->" | "<->" | "--" | "==>"
Endpoint        ::= QualifiedId PortAnchor?
PortAnchor      ::= ("." Side ("@" FloatLit | "(" AttrBody ")")?)
                  | ("#" Ident)


Side            ::= "N" | "E" | "S" | "W"
ZoneDecl        ::= "zone" Ident StringLit? "around" RefList BlockOrAttrs StmtEnd
AlignDecl       ::= "align" ("row" | "col") RefList AttrList? StmtEnd
DistributeDecl  ::= "distribute" ("row" | "col") RefList AttrList? StmtEnd
FlowDecl        ::= "flow" Ident StringLit? "{" EdgeChain* "}" StmtEnd


LayoutBlock     ::= "@layout" ("(" Ident ")")? "{" LayoutRule* "}" StmtEnd
LayoutRule      ::= (QualifiedId | "(" Endpoint EdgeOp Endpoint ")") Annotation+ StmtEnd
